Always check your tenancy agreement, inventory and any written instructions from your landlord or letting agent. What is included in an end of tenancy clean? A thorough end of tenancy clean usually covers every accessible room and surface, including kitchens, bathrooms, bedrooms, living areas, hallways, storage spaces, cupboards, appliances and floors. The aim is to return the property to the same standard of cleanliness recorded at the beginning of the tenancy, allowing for reasonable wear and tear. The exact scope varies. Carpet cleaning, upholstery cleaning, exterior windows, balconies, garages, gardens and specialist stain treatment may not be included in a standard service unless they are requested. Ask for a written checklist before booking so you know what is covered. Before you start cleaning Read the tenancy agreement and check-in inventory. Review move-in photographs and note the original condition. Remove personal belongings, rubbish and unwanted food. Defrost the freezer safely if it must be cleaned. Arrange access to water, electricity and lighting until the clean is finished. Gather suitable products, cloths, vacuum tools, mop heads and protective gloves. Test products on an inconspicuous area before using them on delicate materials. Leave enough drying time for carpets, upholstery and freshly cleaned surfaces. Professional tip: Clean from the top of each room downwards. Dusting high surfaces first prevents debris from falling onto floors that have already been cleaned. Kitchen cleaning checklist Kitchens usually require the most time because grease, food residue and limescale can build up gradually. Empty cupboards and appliances before cleaning, and work in a logical order. Cupboards, drawers and surfaces Remove crumbs, liners and belongings from every cupboard and drawer. Wipe internal shelves, corners, doors, handles and hinges. Degrease worktops, splashbacks and wall tiles. Clean around small appliances and behind movable items. Remove marks from doors, frames, switches and sockets where safe. Oven, hob and extractor Clean oven shelves, trays, internal panels and the door glass. Remove grease and burnt food from the hob and surrounding edges. Clean control knobs without soaking electrical components. Degrease the extractor hood and clean or replace filters where appropriate. Check the space beneath and beside freestanding cookers when accessible. Fridge, freezer and other appliances Empty, defrost and dry the fridge and freezer if required. Clean shelves, drawers, seals, handles and external surfaces. Leave appliance doors slightly open after cleaning to prevent odours. Clean the washing machine drawer, seal, filter area and exterior. Remove food residue from the dishwasher filter, seals and edges. Clean the microwave inside, outside and underneath. Sink, taps and floor Remove limescale and polish taps without damaging the finish. Clean the plughole, overflow and edges around the sink. Wipe skirting boards, kickboards, radiator surfaces and bins. Vacuum edges and corners before mopping the floor. Bathroom and toilet checklist Bathrooms are commonly inspected for soap residue, limescale, mould, hair and odours. Give products enough contact time to work, but never mix cleaning chemicals. Descale taps, shower fittings, shower screens and tile edges. Clean the bath, shower tray, basin and surrounding sealant. Remove hair and residue from accessible plugholes. Clean and disinfect the toilet bowl, seat, hinges, base and surrounding floor. Polish mirrors and glass without leaving streaks. Wipe cabinets, shelves, handles and exposed pipework. Clean extractor covers carefully and remove surface dust. Wash tiles, skirting boards, doors and floors. Check corners and ceiling edges for visible mould or dust. Never mix bleach with acidic products, limescale removers or ammonia. Dangerous fumes can be produced. Follow product labels and ventilate the room. Bedroom checklist Dust wardrobes, shelves, drawers and internal storage spaces. Wipe doors, handles, frames, switches, sockets and skirting boards. Clean mirrors and internal window glass. Vacuum beneath furniture if it can be moved safely. Dust radiators, pipes, lampshades and accessible high surfaces. Remove cobwebs from ceilings and corners. Vacuum carpets slowly, including edges and doorway areas. Mop hard floors and allow them to dry fully. Living room and dining area checklist Dust shelves, fitted units, picture rails and window ledges. Clean tables, chairs and furniture supplied with the tenancy. Vacuum sofas and cushions if they belong to the property. Check beneath cushions and furniture for crumbs or small items. Clean fireplace surfaces according to the material and tenancy instructions. Remove fingerprints from glass, doors and polished surfaces. Vacuum carpet edges and mop hard flooring. Hallways, stairs and entrances Dust banisters, rails, ledges and light fittings. Wipe the front door internally, including handles and letterbox edges. Remove marks from accessible walls without damaging the paint. Vacuum stair edges, corners and beneath removable mats. Clean storage cupboards and utility areas. Check entrance areas for cobwebs, dust and debris. Windows, doors and detailed areas Empty properties often reveal marks that were less visible while furniture and curtains were in place. Pay attention to finishing details throughout the home. Clean internal window glass, frames and sills. Dust blinds and curtain rails where included in the tenancy. Wipe door panels, tops, handles and frames. Clean light switches, sockets and thermostat surfaces carefully. Dust skirting boards throughout the property. Remove cobwebs from ceiling corners. Clean radiators, including accessible gaps and the floor beneath. Check behind doors and around furniture footprints. Do carpets need professional cleaning? Carpets should normally be returned to the cleanliness standard shown in the inventory. Vacuuming may be enough when they are clean and free from staining, but a deeper clean may be appropriate where there are visible marks, odours, heavy traffic areas or pet residue. Check whether carpet cleaning is included in your chosen end of tenancy package. Allow enough drying time before the final inspection, and remember that not every stain can be removed safely. Areas commonly missed during move-out cleaning The top of kitchen cupboards and wardrobes. Oven door edges, shelves and extractor filters. Behind and beneath movable appliances. Washing machine seals and detergent drawers. Dishwasher filters and door edges. Toilet bases, hinges and pipework. Window frames, tracks and sills. Skirting boards, door tops and radiator gaps. Light fittings, switches and socket surrounds. Inside cupboards, drawers and built-in storage. Final inspection before returning the keys Once the cleaning is complete, leave the property for a short time and then inspect it again with fresh eyes. Open curtains and use natural light where possible. Walk through the property using the original inventory. Check every cupboard, drawer, appliance and storage area. Look at floors from different angles for dust, hair or streaks. Confirm that rubbish, food and personal items have been removed. Check for cleaning-product residue and strong chemical odours. Take clear, dated photographs after the clean. Keep your invoice and any written cleaning checklist. When should you hire a professional cleaner? Professional help can be useful when time is limited, the property is large, the oven or bathrooms require intensive cleaning, carpets need specialist equipment, or you want a documented service before the final inspection. Before booking, provide accurate information about the size and condition of the property. Ask what is included, whether cleaning products and equipment are supplied, whether carpets and appliances are separate services, and how any re-clean policy works. It normally includes accessible rooms, surfaces, cupboards, appliances, bathrooms, floors, skirting boards, doors, switches and detailed areas. The key responsibility is normally to return the property to the same standard of cleanliness recorded at the start, allowing for fair wear and tear. Whether professional cleaning is necessary depends on the condition of the property and the tenancy requirements. How long does end of tenancy cleaning take? It depends on the property size, condition, number of bathrooms, appliance build-up and any additional services. A small flat may take several hours, while a larger or heavily used property may require most of a day or longer. Is oven cleaning usually included? Many end of tenancy services include basic or detailed oven cleaning, but this should be confirmed before booking. Heavy grease, specialist appliances and removable parts may affect the time and price. Should the property be empty before the cleaners arrive? An empty property is usually easier and faster to clean.
